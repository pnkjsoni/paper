Patent Agent Exam: Traps from the 2010–2026 papers, the Act and the rule amendments

Next sitting is tentatively January 2027, per the 2026 results notice. The scan covers every paper and key you uploaded, the Patents Act, the 2024 and 2025 rule amendments, the Jan Vishwas Act extract and the Tribunal Reforms extract. Where I quote a "key" answer, it's the 2025/2026 official key as I read it. Parts of the 2025 key PDF are badly formatted, so re-verify any letter you plan to memorise.

1. Biggest trap: old papers and old answers are now wrong

Old belief (2010–2023 papers) Current position
Appeals go to the IPAB IPAB abolished (Tribunals Reforms Act 2021, in force 4.4.2021). Appeals go to the High Court under s.117A, within 3 months. Revocation and rectification are before the HC.
RFE within 48 months 31 months from priority date or filing date, whichever is earlier (Rule 24B, amended 15.3.2024). Applications filed before 15.3.2024 keep 48 months. The 2025 paper tests exactly this: filed 19.02.2024 gives 19.02.2028, filed 20.03.2024 gives 20.10.2026.
Form 3 within 6 months of filing Within 3 months of the first FER (Rule 12(2), 2024). The Controller can ask for a fresh Form 3 within 2 months, and delay can be condoned by up to 3 months via Form 4.
Form 27 annually Once per block of 3 financial years, filed within 6 months of the block's end. No fee. The revised form asks whether the patent is available for licensing. Every patentee and licensee files; one form can cover related patents of the same patentee. "Once in the patent's lifetime" is false.
Grace period 6 months 12 months under s.31, claimed via Form 31 (Rule 29A). It covers only s.31(a)–(d): notified exhibition, publication from it, use by others without consent, and a learned-society paper. Your own LinkedIn or social-media post or a newspaper article is not covered. The 2026 paper has this one.
Renewal advance-payment discount 20% 10%, for 4 or more years paid electronically (Rule 80(3)). 20% is a distractor.
Penalties are imprisonment Jan Vishwas Act (w.e.f. 1.8.2024): ss.120, 122 and 123 are now civil penalties, and s.121 is omitted. s.120 is up to ₹10 lakh plus ₹1,000 per day. s.122(1) is up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹1,000 per day. s.122(2) is the lesser of 0.5% of turnover or ₹5 crore. s.123 is up to ₹5 lakh plus ₹1,000 per day. Ss.118 and 119 (secrecy contravention, false register entries) remain criminal. Non-compliance with an adjudication or appeal order within 90 days gives a fine of ₹1 lakh or up to 1 year's imprisonment, or both (s.124B(6)).
s.4 atomic energy bar Amended by the SHANTI Act 2025. The 2026 paper asks for the blanks: Atomic Energy Act 1962, s.20(1), and SHANTI Act s.38. Distractors are s.21 and 1961. Your Act PDF still shows the old s.4. Also check ss.39 and 65 for consequential changes.
Evidence Act in adjudication The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023, and the adjudicating officer is not bound by it.

2. Adjudication and forms: version collision

  • The March 2024 amendments used Form 31 for the complaint and Form 32 for the appeal. This collided with Form 31 = Grace period.
  • The Nov 2025 rules (G.S.R. 865(E)) replaced Chapter XIVA. Now Form 32 is the complaint and Form 33 is the appeal. Both are filed electronically, and complaint is by "any person".
  • 2026 key: a complaint against an unregistered person posing as a patent agent is filed via Form 32, electronically, by any person. It is not limited to a "person aggrieved", and it is not Form 33.
  • Timelines: show-cause notice of at least 7 days; inquiry finished within 3 months of the Rule 107C(3) notice; appeal within 60 days of receipt of the order. Appeal disposal is "ordinarily" 6 months under the rule but 60 days under s.124B(5), so read the question for Act or Rule. The appellate authority sits at least one rank above the adjudicating officer.

3. Wording traps ("deemed ___")

  • Deemed abandoned applies to:
    • no complete specification within 12 months (s.9(1)), and
    • failure to comply within the s.21 period.
  • Deemed to have been abandoned applies to contravention of secrecy or s.39 (s.40). Post-facto permission is not available. The 2025 key is "deemed abandoned under s.40", with the Form 25 option being the distractor.
  • Deemed withdrawn applies to no RFE (s.11B(4)) and to a PCT application that never enters the national phase.
  • Deemed revoked applies to a patentee who doesn't contest an opposition and to s.66 public-interest revocation.
  • Section 66 requires the Central Government, "mischievous to the State or generally prejudicial to the public", a hearing, and a Gazette declaration. "State Government", "prejudicial" alone, or "High Court" are distractors.
  • Revocation under s.64 is on petition of a person interested or the Central Government, or by counter-claim. The Controller cannot revoke suo motu on the biological-source ground. Revocation is not before the Controller except under ss.65, 85 and 63.

4. Number and date traps

  • Patent term: 20 years from the filing date. For PCT-NP it is the international filing date, not the priority date and not the NP entry date.
    • Convention and ordinary applications filed the same day expire on the same date.
    • A PCT-NP with an earlier IFD expires first, not last. (2026: Statement I true, Statement II false.)
    • A patent of addition co-terminates with the main patent and pays no renewal fees.
  • PCT:
    • Chapter II demand: 3 months from ISR/WO or 22 months from priority, whichever is later.
    • Art. 19 amendments: 2 months.
    • National phase: 31 months.
    • The 2026 timeline key is A=12, B=16, C=18, D=22, E=28, F=30/31.
  • Priority date for a combined claim: where a claim is A + B and B was first disclosed in the later application, the date is the later one (15/06/2024 in the 2026 question). Don't apply the "earlier date" rule of s.11(5) here.
  • Publication: 18 months from priority, or on cessation of secrecy, whichever is later. In the 2025 scenario that is one month from 01/12/2024, not from 06/06/2024.
  • Renewal:
    • Due before the end of year 2, for year 3.
    • Extension of up to 6 months via Form 4.
    • Restoration (Form 15) within 18 months from cessation, which is after the extended period. In the 2024 scenario, 14 months late is restorable but 25 months late is not.
    • Opposition to restoration is only on "not unintentional" or "undue delay".
  • Post-dating (s.17(1)): needs the applicant's request and fee, maximum 6 months, before grant, and not suo motu (2026 key: I, III, IV). A convention application cannot be post-dated beyond the date it could have been filed (s.136(3)).
  • Rule 138: extension or condonation is up to 6 months by Form 4, and requests can be repeated. Rule 137(2) lists the items it cannot extend. The 12-month s.9 period is statutory, so Form 4 won't extend it.
  • Compulsory licence:
    • s.84 can be invoked 3 years from grant, and the reasonable negotiating period is about 6 months, with exceptions.
    • s.85 revocation: 2 years from the first CL order, by the Central Government or any person interested. The Controller can't do it alone. A distractor says "3 years".
  • Opposition:
    • Pre-grant (s.25(1)): any person, Form 7A.
    • Post-grant (s.25(2)): person interested, within 1 year of grant publication, Form 7, e-filing only.
    • Rules 55 and 56 now give 1 month for the Controller's prima facie order and 2 months for replies.
    • A pre-grant opponent has no appeal against the Controller's order.
  • Rectification and name changes:
    • After a successful s.25(2) "obtaining" ground, patent amendment into the opponent's name is by Form 12 within 3 months (s.26). It is not Form 10 or 17.
    • s.28 inventor mention must be requested before grant. After grant the s.28 route is closed, and the 2025 key says "cannot be included now". The new option is the Certificate of Inventorship (Form 8A, Rule 70A).

5. Forms mix-ups to drill

  • Form 3: s.8. Form 4: time. Form 5: inventorship declaration. Form 6: s.20, change of applicant. Form 7 and 7A: oppositions.
  • Form 8: s.28. Form 8A: inventorship certificate. Form 9: early publication. Form 10: s.44, deceased applicant.
  • Form 11: s.51, co-owner directions. Form 12: s.26 and 52 grant. Form 13: s.57 amendment. Form 14: oppose amendment, restoration, surrender, CL terms or clerical correction.
  • Form 15: restoration. Form 16: s.69 title registration.
  • Form 17: CL (ss.84, 91, 92, 92A). Form 18: RFE. Form 18A: expedited, e-filing only, with specific eligibility.
  • Form 19: s.85 revocation. Form 20: s.88(4). Form 21: s.94 termination.
  • Form 22: agent registration. Form 23: agent name restoration (the 2025 option order is shuffled to catch you).
  • Form 24: review or set aside. Form 25: s.39 permission. Form 26: agent authorisation. Form 27: working.
  • Form 28: startup or small-entity claim. Form 29: withdrawal, no fee. Form 30: miscellaneous. Form 31: grace. Forms 32 and 33: complaint and appeal.
  • Sequence-listing fee is per page with a cap (X for small entities, 5X for others). Filing electronically does not waive it, and it applies regardless of Budapest deposit.
  • Startups, small entities, natural persons and educational institutions pay the lower column. Foreign small entities qualify if they meet Indian criteria. Transfer to a large entity means paying the fee difference.

6. Substantive-law traps

  • Prior art:
    • "New invention" is tested on non-publication anywhere, not just India.
    • Under s.13(1)(b) and s.25(1)(c), a prior-filed Indian application published later destroys novelty. A foreign application filed earlier but published later does not. (2026: "only IV".)
    • Anticipation can be arithmetic or implicit. The ophthalmic container example: total 2000 µm minus inner 500 µm gives outer 1500 µm, so there is no novelty.
  • Claim analysis:
    • Combining D1 and D2 can defeat claim 1 as obvious, but a dependent claim carrying a feature D found in no document survives.
    • "Dependent on an obvious claim" does not make it obvious.
    • A claim lacking novelty differs from one only lacking inventive step. Always test novelty first (single document), then inventive step.
  • Definitions (the 2024, 2025 and 2026 papers test these):
    • "Capable of industrial application" is made or used in an industry.
    • Inventive step is (technical advance or economic significance) and non-obviousness.
    • "True and first inventor" excludes importers.
    • Phrases: s.10(4)(a) "fully and particularly"; s.25(1)(g) and 25(2)(g) "sufficiently and clearly"; s.64(1)(h) "sufficiently and fairly".
  • s.3(d): efficacy means therapeutic efficacy (Novartis). A new use or property of a known substance is excluded. Use of a known process is allowed only if it gives a new product or uses a new reactant.
  • Medical methods: s.3(i) bars method of treatment claims (relevant to the Alzheimer antibody draft). Claim the antibody by CDR sequences, with sequence listing, plus the preparation process. 3(k) bars software "per se", so the AI air-purifier claims must recite hardware (sensor, controller, valves and filter bank) and an adaptive flow path.
  • Biological material: deposit under Budapest Treaty no later than filing date. Disclosure of source and geographic origin is a ground for pre-grant opposition, post-grant opposition and revocation.
  • Patent of addition:
    • It can be filed on the same date as the main application (s.54(3)), not only later.
    • Grant only after the main grant.
    • No inventive-step objection based on the main invention (s.56).
    • An independent patent filed earlier than the "main" cannot be converted.
    • A PoA on another PoA is false per the keys.
    • More than one PoA per main invention is allowed.
  • Divisionals: any time before grant, even suo motu (Rule 13(2A) now allows divisionals of divisionals and of provisional-disclosed inventions). Not only after FER, and the filing date is the parent's.
  • Entitlement: an employer with an assignment clause is the applicant. The legal heir does not override it. There is no Controller "determination" step under s.6.
  • Co-owners (s.50–51):
    • Each can work the patent for their own benefit.
    • No licence or assignment without the others' consent.
    • Controller directions on application (Form 11).
  • Licence clauses (s.140): tie-ins, exclusive grant-back, challenge bars and package licensing are void. So a "no validity challenge" clause is invalid, while exclusive licensees' standing to seek revocation is a separate question.
  • Amendments: only by disclaimer, correction or explanation (s.59). Cannot be opposed after grant except by notice under s.57(4) for the amendment itself, within the prescribed time, by a person interested.

7. Enforcement traps

  • Suit forum: a District Court at minimum. With a counter-claim for revocation, the suit moves to the High Court.
  • No infringement suit before grant (s.11A(7) proviso). Damages are reckoned from publication date for the pre-grant period, not from filing.
  • s.111: "Patented" without the patent number does not give constructive awareness, so damages can be refused. Injunctions are still available.
  • s.106: groundless threats. A mere notification of a patent's existence is not a threat. Relief is declaration, injunction and damages, unless the defendant proves infringement. s.105 non-infringement declaration requires a prior written request, and validity cannot be raised.
  • Burden shift (s.104A) applies to process patents for new products or where there's substantial likelihood.
  • Research is exempt only under s.47(3). The "always infringement" statement is a distractor.
  • Exclusive licensee (s.109): can sue for infringement after the licence date, and the patentee is added as defendant with no costs unless they appear.
  • s.84 licensee (s.110): must first call on the patentee, with a 2-month wait.
  • Not appealable: extension-of-time orders (s.81), secrecy directions (ss.37 and 41). s.117A(2) lists the appealable ones, and 92A is not in it.

8. PCT, convention and foreign filing

  • s.39: a PCT application filed at RO/IN by an Indian resident still needs s.39 permission (2026 key: "Yes"). The first-filing-in-India-six-weeks route avoids it.
  • Convention: 12 months from the earliest basic application. Cannot extend by Form 4.
  • Competent ISAs for Indian applicants: the 2025 key treats Japan as the non-competent one (Australia, Austria and Sweden are competent). Re-verify against the current notified list.
  • WIPO treaty on GR/TK (2024): the disclosure requirement is mandatory for applicants, not optional.
    • The question says "optional", so that statement is false.
    • Patent offices have no duty to verify.
    • Grace of 12 months for pre-existing applications is also false.
    • 15 ratifications are needed, and entry into force is 3 months after.

9. Designs

  • Article means separately manufactured and sold, so a non-removable built-in dashboard fails, and a bottle cap sold separately passes. A natural stone sculpture with no industrial application fails.
  • Grace for exhibitions: designs grace is 6 months, patents is 12. So "same duration" is false.
  • Copyright: design copyright term is 10 years plus 5. Under the Copyright Act, copyright ends once a design is applied to more than 50 articles by industrial process.
  • Classes: one design, one class. A design may cover any or all articles in a class. Class decisions and doubtful "sets" are for the Controller, not the proprietor. Registered-design reports are not open to inspection. There are 31 classes plus a miscellaneous class.
  • Cancellation (s.19): person interested. Grounds include prior registration or publication anywhere (US publication counts), not new or original, and not a design (e.g. contains a property mark). The 2025 key: "all of the above".
  • A design application that is abandoned can be re-filed, and the earlier one is not prior art. Earlier registration prejudices any later similar design, whoever the applicant.
  • Form 10 assignment fee for two designs is per design, but the second is cheaper than the first (2026 key, Y < X).
  • Who can file: cancellation, person interested; rectification, person aggrieved; existence of registration, any person; inspection, any person.

10. Patent agent traps

  • Qualifications: Indian citizen, 21 years, science, engineering or technology degree, passed exam. A separate route exists for 10-year ex-examiners and controllers.
  • Applicant can draft and appear personally. A non-agent advocate can attend a hearing only (s.132).
  • s.129: companies cannot practise as patent agents. s.131(2): the Controller must refuse an agent who has no residence or place of business in India.
  • Annual continuance fee is paid each 1 April. Restoration is Form 23 plus the continuation fee.

11. Paper II (drafting) traps

  • The 2026 Paper II has no internal choice: all of Parts A, B and C are compulsory. Plan the 20-mark specification and the 10-markers.
  • Cite section, rule and form for every procedural answer. Most marks are lost on missing form numbers or fees.
  • Specification structure: title, abstract (max 150 words), field, background, summary, drawings, detailed description with best mode, claims, and a justification where the question asks for one.
  • Claims: at least one product and one process independent claim, with dependents under each. Keep a single inventive concept (s.10(5)). Include the distinguishing feature over the closest prior art in the independent claim. Don't claim "AI" or "algorithm" per se, and don't claim method of treatment.
  • Scenario answers: use the time periods from the Rules in force, not from the old papers.
  • Novelty and inventiveness replies: identify the closest document, map features, say what's missing, and argue technical effect. For the ointment table, check ranges and combined features against D1–D4. A narrow prior example inside a claimed range anticipates the range claim.
